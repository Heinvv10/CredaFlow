# Kenya lending market structure and LMS competitive landscape (as of Nov 2026)

Scope note: 17 searches/fetches. Most figures come from Kenyan press that reports CBK/SASRA/Safaricom releases rather than the primary PDFs. Where only vendor marketing or a single source backs a claim, it is flagged. Stale data (pre-2025) is flagged.

## Lender segments and counts (DCPs, MFBs, SACCOs, asset finance, banks' digital lending)

### Takeaway
The largest addressable pool of independent, software-buying lenders is the ~281 CBK-licensed digital credit providers (DCPs) plus 357 SASRA-regulated SACCOs (61 large-tier hold ~78% of assets). Microfinance banks are few (14) and loss-making. Telco/bank digital lending (Fuliza, M-Shwari, KCB M-Pesa) is huge but closed, in-house, and not a buyer.

### Cited Findings
**Digital credit providers (DCPs, non-deposit-taking, CBK-licensed)**
- 30 Sep 2026: CBK licensed 29 more DCPs, bringing total to **281**; >900 applications received since 2022; others still pending, mostly awaiting documents — [Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/cbk-approves-29-more-digital-lenders-n391183); CBK primary release: [CBK press release, 29 additional DCPs](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/uploads/press_releases/366319113_Press%20Release%20-%20Licensing%20of%2029%20Additional%20Digital%20Credit%20Providers.pdf)

**SACCOs (SASRA-regulated)**
- 2025: 357 regulated SACCOs; 61 large-tier (assets >KSh5bn) = 17% of SACCOs but 77.62% of assets; gross loans KSh948.67bn (2024: KSh845.11bn); deposits KSh832.74bn; deposit-taking SACCO assets KSh1.07tn; non-withdrawable DT-SACCO assets KSh141.36bn — [The Kenya Times, SASRA 2025 report coverage](https://thekenyatimes.com/business/sacco-lending-nears-ksh1-trillion-as-property-sector-attracts-biggest-share/)
- Net loans KSh887.67bn end-Dec 2025; total SACCO assets KSh1.21tn — [Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/saccos-deposits-rise-11-12pc-to-sh832-74bn-as-assets-hit-sh1-21tn/); [SACCO Review](https://saccoreview.co.ke/sasra-report-sacco-total-assets-hit-ksh-1-21-trillion-mark/)
- Quarterly stats give KSh948.31bn gross loans (minor conflict with annual report) — [The Standard](https://thestandard.ke/business/amp/business/article/2001542595/sacco-gross-loans-surpass-sh900b-mark)
- Employers failed to remit ~KSh4.2bn of check-off SACCO contributions (payroll-deduction risk) — [Business Today](https://businesstoday.co.ke/employers-withhold-ksh4-2b-sacco-contributions/); [The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/business/kenya/2025-09-26-employers-fail-to-remit-sh42-billion-sacco-contributions-on-tough-economy-1)
- Non-regulated (non-deposit-taking) SACCOs under the Commissioner for Co-operatives: count not found (see Gaps).

**Asset finance / device / boda boda**
- M-KOPA Kenya: KSh207bn credit unlocked, 4.8m customers (Nov 2025, company claim) — [M-KOPA newsroom](https://m-kopa.com/newsroom/m-kopa-kenya-unlocks-kes-207-billion-in-credit-as-4-8-million-customers-report-rising-digital-and-financial-inclusion); >10,000 e-motorbikes financed by Sep 2026 — [TechTrends KE](https://techtrendske.co.ke/2026/09/07/m-kopa-electric-motorcycles-kenya/); FMO $30m debt to M-KOPA Kenya Mobility — [Launchbase Africa](https://launchbaseafrica.com/2026/07/23/fmo-backs-m-kopas-e-mobility-spinout-with-30m-green-debt-package/); M-KOPA turned a profit (group) — [AllAfrica](https://allafrica.com/stories/202510080005.html)
- Watu Credit described as largest boda boda lender by volume, Mogo its main competitor (affiliate-commission site, low reliability) — [Paybill KE](https://paybillke.com/guides/boda-boda-financing-real-cost-kenya-2026)

**Banks' / telco digital lending (not buyers; competitors for borrowers)**
- Fuliza disbursements FY to Mar 2026: ~KSh1.47tn (+49%), 17.7m customers — [Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/fuliza-loans-jump-49pc-to-sh1-47tn/); [Business Today](https://businesstoday.co.ke/safaricoms-fuliza-loans-rise-49-to-ksh1-47t/)
- KCB M-Pesa disbursements KSh73.8bn (+30.3%); M-Shwari KSh95.1bn and Timiza KSh28.5bn (single source) — [People Daily](https://peopledaily.digital/business/fuliza-powers-safaricoms-lending-growth-as-disbursements-hit-ksh1-47t/amp)

### Inferences
- DCP licensing roughly tripled the formal count in under two years; many of the 281 are small, newly licensed firms that need compliant tooling (CBK pricing approval, disclosure, CRB, complaints) — the closest analogue to CredaFlow's NCA lender buyer.
- Large-tier SACCOs (61) already run core banking systems; CredaFlow is a lending layer, not a deposit core, so SACCOs are a weaker fit unless positioned as a loan-origination/collections front end.
- MFBs need prudential core banking (deposits) and are shrinking; poor fit.
- Asset financers (Watu, Mogo, M-KOPA) are large, venture-funded and almost certainly run in-house stacks (not confirmed).

### Gaps

- Count/size of logbook lenders and check-off (payroll) lenders not found; Mogo/Watu DCP licence status not confirmed.

## Market size: digital credit volumes, MSME gap, NPLs, consolidation

### Takeaway
Licensed DCPs disbursed ~KSh165bn across 9.6m loans (cumulative reporting as of Aug 2026), dwarfed by Fuliza's KSh1.47tn/yr. MSME credit-gap estimates range KSh2.2–3.3tn. Bank NPLs remain elevated (~15%). Tala is restructuring but not exiting.

### Cited Findings
- As of Aug 2026 licensed DCPs had granted 9,596,509 loans worth KSh165.1bn (CBK; period basis unclear, likely cumulative/reporting window) — [Citizen Digital](https://citizen.digital/article/cbk-approves-29-more-digital-lenders-n391183); earlier ~KSh150bn — [Business Today](https://businesstoday.co.ke/cbk-licenses-25-digital-credit-providers-as-virtual-loans-soar-to-ksh150-billion/)
- FinAccess 2024: 8.8% of adults borrowed from digital lenders (1.7% in 2021); overall credit usage 64.0% — [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/markets/capital-markets/digital-loans-uptake-up-five-fold-on-central-bank-oversight-4860940); [FSD Kenya](https://www.fsdkenya.org/blogs-publications/2024-finaccess-household-survey-key-insights-into-kenyas-financial-landscape/); primary: [CBK FinAccess 2024 report](https://www.centralbank.go.ke/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/2024-FINACCESS-HOUSEHOLD-SURVEY-MAIN-REPORT.pdf)
- Most common credit sources 2024: Hustler Fund 28.9%, shopkeepers 25.0%, Fuliza 18.4% — [The Star](https://www.the-star.co.ke/news/infographics/2025-03-19-credit-trends-in-kenya-in-2024-where-do-you-mostly-borrow-from)
- MSME gap: World Bank/IFC ~KSh2.2tn, Kenya Bankers Association ~KSh2.5tn — [Capital FM](https://capitalfm.africa/the-sh2-5-trillion-financing-gap-holding-back-kenyan-businesses/); MSME policy review KSh3.3tn (needs ~KSh4tn, banks lend ~KSh700bn) — [The Standard](https://www.standardmedia.co.ke/business/article/2001550982/msmes-face-sh3-3-trillion-credit-gap-as-expansion-plans-stall); Ruto ~KSh3tn (2026) — [Eastleigh Voice](https://eastleighvoice.co.ke/news/372961/access-to-affordable-credit-remains-biggest-msme-challenge-says-ruto)
- Bank NPL ratio: 17.6% Aug 2025 → 15.4% Dec 2025 → 15.6% Mar 2026 → 15.3% May 2026; CBK rate cut to 8.75% (Feb 2026) — [Khusoko](https://khusoko.com/2026/02/13/kenyas-banking-sector-stable-as-moodys-and-cbk-highlight-resilience/); [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/bank-loan-defaults-dip-sh40bn-on-lower-rates-5581436); Moody's warns >16% possible — [Zawya](https://www.zawya.com/en/economy/africa/kenyan-banks-project-surge-in-bad-loans-on-impact-of-geopolitical-tensions-a6gfzknu)
- Tala: June 2026 global reorganisation cutting up to 10% of Kenya staff; Kenya ops continue; 28 cut April 2025; break-even target early 2026 unconfirmed — [TechCabal](https://techcabal.com/2026/06/25/tala-lays-off-staff-in-global-reorganisation/); [Business Daily](https://www.businessdailyafrica.com/bd/corporate/companies/tala-to-lay-off-10pc-of-its-staff-in-kenya-5508546); further cuts July 2026 — [Kenyan Wall Street](https://kenyanwallstreet.com/tala-cuts-additional-jobs-kenya). Ecofin frames it as "pulling work out of Kenya" — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/news-finances/2606-56843-tala-is-pulling-work-out-of-kenya-and-that-matters-more-than-the-100-jobs-its-cutting)

### Inferences
- The DCP segment is consolidating at the top (Tala trimming) while the licensed tail grows: more small lenders, thin margins — price-sensitive buyers.
- KSh165bn DCP volume vs KSh1.47tn Fuliza shows independent lenders compete in niches (SME, asset, check-off) rather than nano-loans.

### Gaps
- Branch, Zenka, Okash performance/exits 2025–26: not searched successfully; no data.
- DCP-sector NPL rate not found.
- Whether the KSh165.1bn is cumulative since licensing or a period figure is unclear.

## What software lenders use today; vendors, pricing, local presence

### Takeaway
The Kenyan market is crowded at the low end with local SaaS LMS vendors priced at KES 5,000–8,000/month (~US$40–60), and at the SACCO/MFB core end by Craft Silicon (Bankers Realm), Kwara, Intrasoft, Lanstar and others. International LMS (TurnKey Lender, Mambu, Lendsqr, Loandisk) are present via a few named clients. No market-share data exists publicly.

### Cited Findings
**SACCO / MFB cores**
- Craft Silicon (Nairobi-based): Bankers Realm SACCO and Core Microfinance products; Elma mobile banking; claims Safaricom Sacco and ICT Women Enterprise Fund as clients — [Craft Silicon SACCO](https://www.craftsilicon.com/industries/sacco/); [BR Core Microfinance](https://www.craftsilicon.com/products/br-core-microfinance/)
- Kwara acquired IRNET from KUSCCO (2023), partnered with the SACCO union; Kwara software served 120+ SACCOs since 2019 (stale, 2023) — [Hapakenya](https://hapakenya.com/2023/01/12/kwara-enters-deal-to-wholly-acquire-irnet-from-kuscco-ltd); [Disrupt Africa](https://disrupt-africa.com/2023/01/16/kenyan-fintech-startup-kwara-acquires-software-company-partners-sacco-union/)
- Lanstar Fine-Xtreme deployed at NSSF SACCO (2024) — [AppsRunTheWorld](https://www.appsruntheworld.com/customers-database/customers/view/nssf-sacco-kenya)

### Inferences
- CredaFlow would face a two-sided squeeze: cheap local SaaS (~US$40–60/month) with M-Pesa built in, and entrenched SACCO cores. Its differentiation would have to be compliance depth (CBK DCP rules, CRB, audit), asset finance/HP, AI and white-label portal/website — and M-Pesa Daraja integration would be table stakes (CredaFlow is built on Stitch/debit orders; not confirmed to support M-Pesa).

- What the large DCPs (Tala, Branch, Watu, Mogo) run: assumed in-house, unconfirmed.

## Buyer pain points

### Takeaway
Vendor messaging centres on M-Pesa Paybill reconciliation, automated collections reminders, CRB-ready repayment data and CBK/SASRA reporting; fraud is rarely addressed.

### Cited Findings
- "Never reconciling M-Pesa by hand again" cited as the biggest win; auto Paybill matching to ledger; day-one missed-instalment flags + SMS reminders; CRB-exportable histories; CBK non-deposit lender obligations: CBK-approved pricing, full cost disclosure incl. APR, fair collections, complaints handling, explainable automated decisions; SASRA statutory returns — [LendPoint](https://www.lendpoint.africa/blog/loan-management-system-in-kenya-complete-guide-for-saccos-mfis-2026/)
- Craft Silicon's SACCO pitch: automating reconciliations, receipting, interbank transfers — [Craft Silicon](https://www.craftsilicon.com/industries/sacco/)
- Check-off remittance failures (KSh4.2bn withheld by employers) are a collections pain for payroll lenders/SACCOs — [Business Today](https://businesstoday.co.ke/employers-withhold-ksh4-2b-sacco-contributions/)

### Inferences
- CredaFlow's audit trail, complaints and compliance modules map onto CBK DCP obligations; M-Pesa reconciliation is the gap that buyers rank first.

### Gaps
- No independent buyer survey on LMS pain points found; fraud evidence absent.

## South African lending/fintech software vendors in Kenya

### Takeaway
No evidence found of a South African LMS vendor succeeding in Kenya; the visible SA moves are payments (Ukheshe) and banks/financiers, and flows mostly run Kenya → SA (M-KOPA, Cellulant, FinPlus).

### Cited Findings
- Ukheshe (SA) partnered with KCB for card issuing in East Africa (payments, not lending) — [IOL](https://www.iol.co.za/technology/fintech/ukheshe-will-issue-cards-across-east-africa-with-support-from-kcb-fd50c5e8-9f53-46a8-a595-ec88b66df2ac)
- Fin (formerly Finclusion), founded by South Africans, operates lending in Kenya, SA, Tanzania — per search summary of [MicroCapital](https://www.microcapital.org/?p=47228) (not opened; unconfirmed)
- Reverse direction: Kenyan FinPlus took its SaaS banking platform to SA (2019, stale) — [Disrupt Africa](https://disrupt-africa.com/2019/04/12/kenyas-finplus-expands-saas-banking-platform-to-sa/); Cellulant expanded to SA — [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/kenya-fintech-cellulant-starts-expansion-push-to-south-africa-1.1853438); Standard Bank arranged $200m debt for M-KOPA — [Ecofin](https://www.ecofinagency.com/finance/1505-44529-fintech-m-kopa-secures-255-mln-initiates-ssa-expansion)

### Inferences
- Absence of SA LMS precedent means no playbook, but also no incumbent SA competitor; local presence and M-Pesa integration appear to be what Kenyan vendors compete on.

### Gaps
- Jumo, Sybrin, Compuscan/Experian, Altron in Kenyan lending software: no results; would need targeted searches.
